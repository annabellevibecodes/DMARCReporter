Alerts

Alert rules run after every import (upload or mailbox fetch) and on a timer (ALERT_EVAL_INTERVAL, default 24h) so time-based rules fire even when no new report arrives. Every firing is recorded on the Alerts page, where it can be acknowledged; when ALERT_WEBHOOK_URL and/or SMTP_HOST are configured, new alerts are also delivered as JSON or email. An alert is identified by rule + domain + key: firing again bumps its count and "last fired" instead of creating a duplicate, and an acknowledged alert re-opens (and is delivered again) only when the condition fires after the acknowledgement. Delivery is limited to once per alert per day.

"Recent" windows default to 48 hours rather than 24 because a reporter's window for a day arrives during the following day.

Most rules watch a metric from the metric reference and fire when it crosses a threshold: X3 uses the source labels from S3, X6 the reporter gaps from R2, X7 and X8 the policy change log and consistency check from P3 and P2, X9 the DNS error rate from A8, and X11 the spoof volume from S4. Looking up the metric explains what the alert saw.

X12–X19 do the same for SMTP TLS reporting: X12 watches the success rate from T1, X13–X14 and X17 the result types from T4, X15 the policy divergence and drift from T8, X16 the policy-host errors from T10, X18 the reporter coverage from T12, and X19 the per-node failures from T5. A report excluded by the RFC 8460 §3 signature requirement raises no alert either, so what you see on the TLS page and what alerts fire always agree.

Health Score

The score on the dashboard and domain pages adds six components, each capped at its weight (HEALTH_W_*):

ComponentFormulaWeight
Pass rateDMARC pass rate × weight / 10040
Enforcementp=none 0 · p=quarantine 60% · p=reject 100% of the weight25
Spoof share(100 − spoof share) × weight / 100 — spoof share is the not-in-SPF volume when resolved, otherwise the "neither" share15
Reporter coverage≥3 distinct reporters in the last 7 days → full, 2 → half, else 010
AlertsNo open critical alert → full10
Transport securityTLS success rate (T1) for 70% of the weight, plus TLS reporter coverage (T12) for the rest: ≥2 reporters → full, 1 → half15

A domain with no TLS reports is not marked down for it. When there are no TLS sessions to judge, the transport-security component is left out of the maximum as well as the total, so the score means exactly what it did before the component existed. Absence of evidence and evidence of breakage are different states: a domain nobody reports on keeps its DMARC score out of 100, while a domain whose TLS is actually failing loses points out of 115. The score is shown as a value out of its own maximum for that reason.

TLS reports excluded by the RFC 8460 §3 signature requirement do not count toward the component, the same as everywhere else. A domain whose only TLS reports arrive unsigned therefore scores as "no data" rather than as a failure — the TLS page says how many were held back.

Labels: ≥85% Excellent, ≥65% Good, ≥40% Fair, otherwise Critical — as a share of the maximum in play.

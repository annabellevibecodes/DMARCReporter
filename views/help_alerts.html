Alerts

Alert rules run after every import (upload or mailbox fetch) and on a timer (ALERT_EVAL_INTERVAL, default 24h) so time-based rules fire even when no new report arrives. Every firing is recorded on the Alerts page, where it can be acknowledged; when ALERT_WEBHOOK_URL and/or SMTP_HOST are configured, new alerts are also delivered as JSON or email. An alert is identified by rule + domain + key: firing again bumps its count and "last fired" instead of creating a duplicate, and an acknowledged alert re-opens (and is delivered again) only when the condition fires after the acknowledgement. Delivery is limited to once per alert per day.

"Recent" windows default to 48 hours rather than 24 because a reporter's window for a day arrives during the following day.

Most rules watch a metric from the metric reference and fire when it crosses a threshold: X3 uses the source labels from S3, X6 the reporter gaps from R2, X7 and X8 the policy change log and consistency check from P3 and P2, X9 the DNS error rate from A8, and X11 the spoof volume from S4. Looking up the metric explains what the alert saw.

X12–X19 do the same for SMTP TLS reporting: X12 watches the success rate from T1, X13–X14 and X17 the result types from T4, X15 the policy divergence and drift from T8, X16 the policy-host errors from T10, X18 the reporter coverage from T12, and X19 the per-node failures from T5. X20–X27 cover brand indicators: X20 is the B5 change log, X21 the B10 policy precondition, X22 the B3 selector inventory, X23 and X24 the indicator endpoint and profile checks from B6 and B7, X25 and X26 the evidence document from B8, and X27 the mark agreement from B9. X21 is the one alert here that reports wasted effort rather than breakage — the record is fine, and no receiver will ever look at it. X23–X27 need the active checks, which are switched off by BIMI_VALIDATE=false; a domain those checks have never run for raises none of them, because absence of evidence is not evidence of breakage. X28 and X29 read the <bimi> element of aggregate reports (B12 and B17) and will be silent for most operators for a long time — almost nobody emits it yet, which is what B18 measures. X28 treats a permanent error as critical because of what it permits: an evaluator that classes an error permanent may stop evaluating BIMI for every message from the domain until something changes. X30 is the only alert here built on what a real receiver did rather than on what it reported: it needs a seed mailbox (an account with its purpose set to seed), and it needs the receiver to have passed before, so it fires on a change rather than on a receiver that has never shown the logo. A report excluded by the RFC 8460 §3 signature requirement raises no alert either, so what you see on the TLS page and what alerts fire always agree.

Health Score

The score on the dashboard and domain pages adds seven components, each capped at its weight (HEALTH_W_*):

ComponentFormulaWeight
Pass rateDMARC pass rate × weight / 10040
Enforcementp=none 0 · p=quarantine 60% · p=reject 100% of the weight25
Spoof share(100 − spoof share) × weight / 100 — spoof share is the not-in-SPF volume when resolved, otherwise the "neither" share15
Reporter coverage≥3 distinct reporters in the last 7 days → full, 2 → half, else 010
AlertsNo open critical alert → full10
Transport securityTLS success rate (T1) for 70% of the weight, plus TLS reporter coverage (T12) for the rest: ≥2 reporters → full, 1 → half15
Brand indicatorsThe DMARC policy permitting BIMI (B10) for 40% of the weight, plus the logo and evidence document checking out (B6–B9) for the rest. Scored only for a domain that publishes a record10

A domain with no TLS reports is not marked down for it. When there are no TLS sessions to judge, the transport-security component is left out of the maximum as well as the total, so the score means exactly what it did before the component existed. Absence of evidence and evidence of breakage are different states: a domain nobody reports on keeps its DMARC score out of 100, while a domain whose TLS is actually failing loses points out of 115. The score is shown as a value out of its own maximum for that reason.

TLS reports excluded by the RFC 8460 §3 signature requirement do not count toward the component, the same as everywhere else. A domain whose only TLS reports arrive unsigned therefore scores as "no data" rather than as a failure — the TLS page says how many were held back.

The same applies to BIMI, twice over. A domain that publishes no BIMI record — or that declines to publish one, which is a decision rather than a gap — is not scored for BIMI at all: the weight leaves the maximum, so the score is what it would have been without the component. And a domain that does publish one but whose logo and certificate have not been retrieved (because BIMI_VALIDATE is off, or Enrich has not run) is scored only on the policy half, with the other 60% likewise out of the maximum. An unperformed check is not a failed one.

Labels: ≥85% Excellent, ≥65% Good, ≥40% Fair, otherwise Critical — as a share of the maximum in play.
